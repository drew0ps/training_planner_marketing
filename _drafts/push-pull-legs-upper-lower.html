Push · Pull · Legs · Upper · Lower: five days, every muscle twice

Push, pull, legs, a rest day, then upper and lower. Five sessions train every muscle twice a week, which a five-day push/pull/legs can't do and a four-day upper/lower can't fit as much into.

Who it's for

This hybrid is for an intermediate lifter after muscle with five days a week. A push, pull, legs split run on five days leaves some muscles at once a week; an upper and lower split on four days has fewer sessions to spread the work over. Doing one round of each trains everything twice. Sessions take 50 to 65 minutes.

The workouts

The push, pull and leg days are the heavier ones: a top set and back-offs on the bench press and the squat, weighted pull-ups and rows. The upper and lower days lean on dumbbells, machines and cables, with more supersets, and the lower day has the week's deadlift.

How it progresses

By double progression. The bench press and the squat have top and back-off sets, each climbing in its own range; in the first session, pick a top set you could do for seven, and back-offs about 15 % lighter.

Phases and deloads

Two phases of five weeks, Accumulation and Intensification, each ending with a deload week.

Back, quads and glutes get fifteen or sixteen sets a week, chest and hamstrings twelve, the arms and shoulders seven to nine: inside the usual weekly range for nearly every muscle, which makes it a program you can run for a long time.

Tips

  • Mind the rest day. Day 4 sits between the legs day and the upper day for a reason: the legs get two days before the lower day.
  • Keep the order when you move a day. Change day on Today shows what moves before it does.
