Pyramid sets: heavier with every set

Twelve reps, then ten a little heavier, then eight, then six: every set heavier with fewer reps than the one before. The pyramid is one of the oldest set schemes in the gym, and it still earns its place.

Not published yet · 3 min read

How a pyramid works

The first set is light enough for a lot of reps, and every set after it adds weight and drops reps. Four sets of a bench press pyramid might be 60 kg × 12, 70 × 10, 80 × 8, 90 × 6. The early sets are volume and practice, the last ones heavy work, and every set counts: unlike warm-up sets, they're all taken close to failure.

It's a good fit for the main lift of a session when you want both size and strength from it, and for anyone who likes the rhythm of a climb. The cost is time and fatigue: a four-step pyramid is four hard sets.

Up and down

A pyramid can come back down: up to a top set, then one or two lighter sets for more reps, 90 × 6 and then 70 × 10. The way down is extra volume when you're warm and the heavy set is done, much like back-off sets.

How a pyramid progresses

Each step moves on its own. On linear progression a step goes up once you get all its reps; with a rep range, once its reps reach the top. In TrainingPlanner's Pyramid Upper · Lower, the main lifts are pyramids of 12, 10, 8 and 6, with 5 kg (upper body) or 10 kg (legs) between sets, and each step moves up once you get all its reps.

In the workout, each set of a pyramid starts from what you actually lifted in the set before, plus the step, with that set's reps. Lift less than planned on set two, and set three climbs from there, not from the plan.

Plan one in TrainingPlanner

A pyramid is a kind of set, like a working set or a top set. There are two ways to plan one:

  • Even steps: the number of sets, the first set's reps and weight, how many reps fewer and how much more weight each set. Three sets from 12 reps, two reps fewer and 2.5 kg more each, is the default.
  • Set by set: every set's own weight and reps, typed in one line if you like: "60x12 70x10 80x8 90x6 70x10". Up and down works too.
  1. Open the program's board and tap the exercise.
  2. Tap Add sets and, under Pyramids and intensity techniques, pick Pyramid.
  3. Open it. In Even steps, set the sets, the first set's reps and weight, and the steps.
  4. Or switch to Set by set and type the sets: weight x reps, separated by spaces, then Use these sets.

A deload keeps a pyramid's shape at half the volume: every other set, about 10 % lighter.
