Training methods

Warm-up sets: ramping up to your first heavy set

Don't walk up to a heavy bar cold. A few lighter sets of the same lift, building up to the first real one, get the muscles, the joints and the movement ready. TrainingPlanner works the ramp out for you, plates and all.

Not published yet · 3 min read

Two kinds of warm-up

A general warm-up raises your body temperature and heart rate: five minutes on a bike or a rower, a few minutes of easy movement for the joints you're about to use. It's worth doing before any session, and more so on a cold morning.

A specific warm-up is the lift itself, lighter: a few sets of the exercise you're about to do, building up to the first working weight. It rehearses the movement, wakes up the muscles in the order the lift uses them, and lets you notice a sore shoulder before 100 kg finds it for you.

How to ramp up

Few reps, rising weight, never tiring. The point is to arrive at your first working set fresh. A good ramp for a weight you'll lift for five:

Before a first set of 100 kg
SetWeightReps
Empty bar20 kg8–10
About 40 %40 kg8
About 60 %60 kg5
About 80 %80 kg3
First working set100 kg5

Lighter first sets need fewer steps; a set of 40 kg needs the bar and one step at most. Rest a minute or so between warm-up sets, more before the first working set. Later exercises in the session need less: the muscles are warm, and one or two sets of the new movement are enough.

Warm-up sets don't count as work. They're not taken near failure, they don't build anything, and they shouldn't be in your volume: TrainingPlanner neither plans them as sets nor counts them.

In TrainingPlanner

When a workout starts, the first exercise's card says to warm up first, and shows the ramp up to its first set. Without a ramp, for a light first weight, it says what to do: "A set or two of this exercise with a light weight and easy effort, then build up to your first set." Tap Done or swipe it away when you have.

When the first set's weight is 30 kg or more, its sheet also shows a ramp beside the weight: about 40, 60 and 80 % of it for 8, 5 and 3 reps, rounded to plates you can load, and steps that would repeat a weight left out. The plates for each side of the bar are below it, from the plates you said your gym has.

  1. Start the session from Today. The first exercise asks you to warm up.
  2. Tap Start next set: the sheet shows the ramp up to the weight.
  3. Do the ramp, then the set, and log it as usual. Warm-up sets aren't logged.
  4. Tell the app your plates in Settings › Equipment so every weight is one you can load.

Holds and cardio get no ramp; neither do bodyweight exercises, or sets whose weight you pick in the workout. A program's first weeks often start below 30 kg, so the ramp appears as the weights climb.
