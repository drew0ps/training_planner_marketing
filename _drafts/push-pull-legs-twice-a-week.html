Push · Pull · Legs ×2: six days, a heavy and a volume version of each

Push, pull, legs, heavy; then push, pull, legs again, for volume; one day off. Six sessions a week train every muscle twice, once for strength and once for size, at a volume few other splits reach.

Who it's for

Push · Pull · Legs ×2 is for an experienced lifter after maximum muscle, who can train six days a week and recover from it: sleep, food and a life that allows it. It's a lot of training. If three or four days is what your week holds, Push · Legs · Pull or Upper · Lower will serve you better. Sessions take 50 to 75 minutes.

The workouts

The heavy days open with a top set and back-offs on the bench press, the deadlift and the squat, then two or three more heavy lifts for 6–10 reps. The volume days use machines, cables and dumbbells for 10–20 reps, and finish their exercises with drop sets, rest-pause and myo-reps.

How it progresses

By double progression, each set in its range. The intensity techniques start from the set before them, so they climb with it: a drop set is about 20 % below the weight you just lifted, rest-pause and myo-reps at the same weight.

Phases and deloads

Two phases of five weeks, Accumulation and Intensification, each ending with a deload week. On six days a week the deloads matter: take them.

The quads and the back get over twenty work sets a week, the chest and shoulders sixteen. That's at or over the top of the usual weekly ranges, which is what an advanced program for size looks like. Counted as effective sets, by how hard they were, it's a little less, and the calendar shows each muscle against its range as you go.

Tips

  • Answer the questions after each exercise. At this volume, the three questions keep the sets where you can use them: "too much" takes one away next cycle.
  • Watch your recovery. If readiness keeps coming in low or the same muscles are still recovering on their next day, drop a set or two from the volume days before something drops it for you.
  • Swap with care. Moving a session can put two leg days back to back; Change day shows what moves before it does.
