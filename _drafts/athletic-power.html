Athletic Power: strength that turns into speed

Jumps and cleans while you're fresh, heavy lifts after them, and single-leg and core work to finish. A strength block, then a power block where the same weights move faster: training for the field, the court or the slopes.

Who it's for

Athletic Power is for an athlete, or an experienced lifter who trains like one: someone who wants to jump higher, sprint faster, change direction harder, and has the strength base to build on. It suits an off-season, or the weeks before one. Four days a week, about an hour each.

The workouts

The power days start with explosive work while you're fresh, box jumps and power cleans on the lower day, push presses on the upper, then heavy squats or bench presses for sets of three to five. The strength days are trap-bar deadlifts, front squats, incline presses and rows for four to six, with single-leg work, Nordic curls and anti-rotation core work after.

Week by week

In the first block the main lifts build strength in their sets. In the second, the power block, the squat, the bench press, the incline press and the trap-bar deadlift drop to five fast sets of two to three, at the weight you've built up to: the same load, moved faster.

How it progresses

The power cleans and push presses move by linear progression, but with a rule of their own: add weight only while every rep stays fast. The other lifts climb by double progression, and the jumps by reps, at a height you land softly on.

Start it in TrainingPlanner

  1. Open Programs, tap + and Start from a Template.
  2. Tap Template and pick Athletic Power under Advanced. The picture at the top shows its cycle, phases and deloads.
  3. Its cycle is a week: the lower and upper power days on days 1 and 2, the strength days on 4 and 5. Change the training days if your week needs it, and tap Create.
  4. Start it from Today and pick your weights in the first week; for the cleans and the push press, a weight you can move fast for every rep.

Tips

  • Fast beats heavy. A slow clean is a deadlift with extra steps. Lower the weight before you lose the speed.
  • Count your sport. Practice and matches tire you too. With Pro, other apps' workouts in Apple Health count in your training load; or add a match on Today, and the recovery figure counts it.
  • Learn the clean from a coach. It's a technical lift; the exercise's page explains it, but a coach sees what you can't.
