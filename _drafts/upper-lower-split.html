Upper · Lower: four days, every muscle twice a week

Upper body Monday, legs Tuesday, rest, then upper and lower again, lighter this time. Four sessions train every muscle twice a week, one heavy and one lighter, and each session is short enough to fit before work.

Who it's for

Upper · Lower is for the second year of lifting and beyond: you know the lifts, adding weight every workout has stopped working, and you want to build muscle with a plan that's still simple to follow. Four days a week, about 45 to 50 minutes each. It's what TrainingPlanner's setup recommends to an intermediate lifter who wants muscle on four days a week.

How a cycle looks

A week: upper and lower on days 1 and 2, a rest day, upper and lower again on days 4 and 5, then two days off.

The workouts

The heavy days start with a top set and back-off sets on the bench press and the squat; the light days use dumbbells, rows on a chest support and a trap-bar deadlift, and more supersets. Every muscle gets a heavier day and a lighter one.

How it progresses

By double progression: each set climbs through its rep range at the same weight, then steps up. The top sets move first, in ranges of 5–7 (4–6 for the trap-bar deadlift), the back-offs follow in 8–10. In the first session, pick a top set you could do for seven, and back-offs about 15 % lighter, as the note on the bench press says.

The legs get the most, from the squat, the leg press and the split squats; the upper body is spread evenly between chest, back, shoulders and arms. For more upper-body volume, add a set to the light day's presses and rows; the planner shows when a muscle passes the top of its weekly range.

Tips

  • Keep the light days light. They're lighter by exercise choice, not by effort: take their sets close to failure too.
  • Move days, not sessions. If Tuesday doesn't work, use Change day on Today; the order stays upper, lower, upper, lower.
  • Want it in waves? Apply a periodization model to it; the undulating ones suit a muscle program like this.
