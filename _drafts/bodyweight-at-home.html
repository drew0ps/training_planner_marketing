Bodyweight at Home: strength and muscle with no equipment

A pull-up bar, a chair, a sturdy table and the floor. Three full-body workouts that build real strength and muscle at home, and get harder as you do: more reps first, then a harder version of the move.

Who it's for

Bodyweight at Home is for training at home, in a hotel room or a park, with no weights at all: a pull-up bar (a door frame bar is fine), a chair or a bench, and a sturdy table to row under. It suits a beginner who wants strength and muscle, and anyone who can't get to a gym for a while. Sessions take about 40 minutes.

How a cycle looks

Three workouts, A, B and C, each followed by a rest day: a six-day cycle that slides through the week. If you'd rather keep the same weekdays, change the cycle to seven days when you create it.

How it progresses

With no weight to add, reps do the climbing, by double progression: one more rep on your weakest set each time, until every set reaches the top of its range. Then it's time for a harder variation, and the app says so: "add load or a harder variation". Push-ups go from hands on a bench to the floor to feet on a chair; rows go under a lower table; split squats go to a backpack with books in it.

Too hard to start with? Make it easier the same way, as the notes say: a band or slow lowering for pull-ups, hands on a bench for push-ups, sitting back to a chair for the pistol squat.

Tips

  • Control every rep. Without weight to add, slower reps are the other way to make an exercise harder: two or three seconds on the way down.
  • The Nordic curl is hard. Lowering slowly is the exercise; three good negatives beat six fast falls.
  • Outgrown it? When the hardest variations reach the top of their ranges, Bodyweight Strength adds weighted pull-ups and dips, pistols and handstand push-ups.
