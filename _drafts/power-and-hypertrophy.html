Power & Hypertrophy: heavy compounds and body-part volume in one week

Monday and Tuesday are heavy: rows, bench, squats for sets of three to five. The rest of the week is for size: back and shoulders, legs, chest and arms, for sets of eight to twenty. Strength and muscle in the same week, each with days of its own.

Who it's for

Power & Hypertrophy is for an experienced lifter who wants both: a stronger squat, bench press and row, and more muscle everywhere. Five days a week, an hour or a little more. It's demanding, and it's a good fit for lifters who get bored of either kind of training on its own.

The workouts

The power days, upper and lower, open with heavy compound lifts for three sets of three to five, then a few heavier accessories for six to ten. The hypertrophy days go by body part (back and shoulders, legs, chest and arms) for eight to twenty reps, and finish exercises with drop sets, rest-pause and myo-reps.

How it progresses

Every set climbs by double progression in its own range: the power lifts from three reps to five, then heavier; the hypertrophy work from the bottom of its range to the top. Because the app progresses each exercise from the last time it was done with the same rep target, the hack squat on the power day and on the hypertrophy day each keep their own weights.

The heavy and light days in one week make it a kind of undulating program, which is why its phases are marked undulating.

Start it in TrainingPlanner

  1. Open Programs, tap + and Start from a Template.
  2. Tap Template and pick Power & Hypertrophy under Advanced. The picture at the top shows its cycle, phases and deloads.
  3. Its cycle is a week: the power days on days 1 and 2, the hypertrophy days on 4, 5 and 6. Change the training days if your week needs it, and tap Create.
  4. Start it from Today and pick your weights in the first week: the power lifts something you could do for five, with a rep or two in reserve.

Tips

  • Rest fully on the power days. Three minutes between heavy sets isn't laziness; it's what lets the next set be heavy.
  • Save the techniques for last. A drop set or myo-reps finish an exercise; they don't replace its working sets.
  • Watch the legs. Two leg days, one of them long, add up. If the hypertrophy leg day drags, the recovery figure on Today usually shows why.
