Rep Range Waves: a heavy week, a moderate week, a high-rep week, a deload

One week heavy, one week moderate, one week for high reps, one week easy, then around again a little stronger. The rep range changes every week, so the body never settles into one, and there's always a new number to beat.

Who it's for

Rep Range Waves is for an intermediate lifter after muscle who likes body-part days and gets bored with the same sets every week. Four days a week, 45 to 65 minutes each. It's undulating by the week: what changes from week to week is the rep range, not the exercises.

How it progresses

Each week climbs by double progression against the same week of the wave before: the heavy week against the last heavy week, the high-rep week against the last high-rep week. The app keeps them apart because it compares each set with the last time the exercise was done for the same reps. So every week has a number to beat, and the second and third waves start stronger than the first.

Start it in TrainingPlanner

  1. Open Programs, tap + and Start from a Template.
  2. Tap Template and pick Rep Range Waves under Intermediate. The picture at the top shows its cycle, phases and deloads.
  3. Its cycle is a week: chest and triceps, back and biceps on days 1 and 2, legs, shoulders and abs on 4 and 5. Change the training days if your week needs it, and tap Create.
  4. Start it from Today and pick your weights each week of the first wave: the heavy week's are much heavier than the high-rep week's, and each is kept for the next wave.

Tips

  • The first wave sets the bar. Pick honest weights in each week of it; from the second wave on, the app has a number for every week.
  • The high-rep week hurts. Twelve to fifteen reps on squats and Romanian deadlifts is a different kind of hard. Rest the full time.
  • Can't run it as a repeating plan: the waves are the method, so it's a structured plan only.
