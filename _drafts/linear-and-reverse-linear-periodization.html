Training methods

Linear and reverse linear periodization

The oldest plan in strength training: start with more reps at moderate weights, and get heavier phase by phase until you peak. Turn it around and it's a plan for size and endurance instead.

Not published yet · 3 min read

Linear: from volume to a peak

Linear periodization runs in three phases. A hypertrophy phase of sets of ten at moderate loads builds muscle and work capacity. A strength phase of fives turns that into force on the big lifts. A short peaking phase of doubles at near-maximal loads gets you ready to show it: a test day, a meet, or simply a new best.

"Linear" means the direction: fewer reps and heavier loads, phase after phase, never back. Inside each phase the load climbs too. Here's what TrainingPlanner writes for a linear program of three four-cycle phases, with a deload ending each:

The percentages are of each exercise's one-rep max. With a three-day cycle, that's three sessions a cycle at those numbers, for every exercise the model writes (accessories keep their own sets).

Who it suits

Linear is the model TrainingPlanner chooses for a beginner, unless the goal is muscular endurance, and for general fitness at an intermediate level. It's simple, predictable, and easy to follow: you always know what the next phase is for. Its weakness shows over long programs: by the end of the peaking phase, the muscle from the first phase has had two months without much volume. That's why advanced lifters go to blocks or undulating models, which keep more qualities in play at once.

Reverse linear: from heavy to high reps

Reverse linear runs the other way: strength first (fives at 80 % and up), then hypertrophy (tens at 70 % and up), then endurance (three sets of fifteen at 60 % and up, a minute's rest). Strength built early lets the later phases move heavier weights for their reps, which is what you want when the goal at the end is size or muscular endurance rather than a max.

TrainingPlanner picks reverse linear for muscular endurance, at any level. It also suits a program before a season of a sport where lasting matters more than peaking: rowing, climbing, a long hike.

Use them in TrainingPlanner

  1. In Programs, tap + › Build My Own › Structured Plan, or open one you have.
