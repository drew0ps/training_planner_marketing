Pyramid Upper · Lower: main lifts in pyramids, four days a week

Twelve reps, ten, eight, six, a step heavier each time: the main lifts of every session climb a pyramid. You get volume and heavy work from the same exercise, and four days a week train every muscle twice.

Who it's for

Pyramid Upper · Lower is for an intermediate lifter after size and strength who likes the rhythm of a climb, set after set. Four days a week, about 55 minutes each. It's the same upper and lower split as Upper · Lower, with pyramids in place of top and back-off sets.

The workouts

Each session opens with two main lifts as pyramids: bench press and rows, squats and Romanian deadlifts, incline presses and pulldowns, deadlifts and hack squats. The upper-body pyramids add 5 kg a set, the lower-body ones 10 kg; the deadlift climbs from eight reps down to four. Then accessories, two at a time in supersets.

How it progresses

Each step of a pyramid moves up once you get all its reps, by linear progression. In the workout, each set starts from what you actually lifted on the set before plus the step, with its own reps. Pick the first set's weight in the first workout; one you could lift for about fourteen reps is a good start for a pyramid from twelve. The accessories climb by double progression.

Phases and deloads

Two phases of five weeks, Base and Build, each ending with a deload week; a deload keeps the pyramid's shape at half the sets.

Tips

  • Leave room at the top. The last step should be hard but clean. If the six is a grind, start the pyramid lighter.
  • Warm up into the first step. Twelve reps is light, but the bar still deserves a ramp for heavy lifts like the squat and the deadlift.
  • Change the steps on the exercise's page: fewer sets, smaller jumps, or set by set if you'd rather come back down after the top set.
