Full Body A/B: a first barbell program, every other day

Workout A, a day off, workout B, a day off, and around again. Three sets of five on the big barbell lifts, starting with the empty bar and a step heavier every time you get all your reps. About as simple as a real program gets.

Who it's for

Full Body A/B is a first program: for someone new to the barbell, or back after a long break, who wants to get stronger and learn the five big lifts properly. Two workouts take turns every other day, so it fits around a week that isn't the same every week. You need a barbell, a rack, a bench, plates and something to hang from. A session takes about 45 minutes.

How a cycle looks

The cycle is four days, A, rest, B, rest, and it repeats from the day you start. That's three or four workouts a week, never two days in a row.

  1. Day 1Full Body A
  2. Day 2Rest
  3. Day 3Full Body B
  4. Day 4Rest

The workouts

Both workouts start with the squat. Workout A adds the bench press and the row; workout B the overhead press, one heavy set of deadlifts and chin-ups. Each ends with a little core or back work, which progresses by reps.

How it progresses

The barbell lifts move by linear progression: get all three sets of five and the next time you do that lift it's a step heavier, 2.5 kg for the squat, the bench press and the row, 1.25 kg for the press and 5 kg for the deadlift. Miss a rep and the weight stays until you get them all. The squat is in both workouts, so it climbs every session; the others every other session.

The chin-ups and knee raises climb by reps instead, with double progression: from 5 to 8 chin-ups a set, then harder. Not at five clean chin-ups yet? Use a band, as the note says.

Phases and deloads

Two phases of four weeks, Foundation and Overload. Each ends with a four-day deload cycle: half the sets at about 10 % less weight, before the climb starts again.

The squat and the deadlift make it a lower-body-heavy program, and the row and chin-ups give the back as much work as the legs. The chest and shoulders get one lift each, every other session: enough while the weights are climbing fast.

Start it in TrainingPlanner

  1. Open Programs, tap + and Start from a Template.
  2. Tap Template and pick Full Body A/B under Beginner. The picture at the top shows its cycle, phases and deloads.
  3. Its cycle is four days: Full Body A on day 1, Full Body B on day 3, a rest day after each. Change the training days if your week needs it, and tap Create.
  4. Start it from Today. The squat, bench press and press start at the empty bar, the row at 30 kg and the deadlift at 40 kg; change them in the first workout if you're already past them.

Tips

  • Learn the lifts while they're light. The first weeks are the time to get the squat depth, the bar path and the setup right. Each exercise's page shows how it's done, step by step.
  • Small jumps last longer. If your gym has small plates, tell the app in Settings › Equipment; every weight will be one you can load.
  • Eat and sleep for it. Adding weight every session works only while you recover between them.
  • After eight weeks, run it again from your new weights, or move on to 5×5 Strength for more volume, or Tiered Full Body for heavy and light work in each session.
