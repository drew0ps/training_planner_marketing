Training methods

Drop sets, rest-pause and myo-reps

The last reps of a hard set are the ones that build muscle. Drop sets, rest-pause and myo-reps are three ways of getting more of those reps without starting another full set: more work in less time, at a price in fatigue.

Not published yet · 4 min read

The three techniques

Drop sets

Take a set close to failure, take about 20 % of the weight off, and go again straight away, with no rest. Do it once or twice. The lighter weight lets the tired muscle keep working when the heavier one would have stopped it. Easy on machines, cables and dumbbell racks, where the weight changes in seconds; awkward on a loaded barbell unless someone strips the plates for you.

Rest-pause

Same weight throughout: take a set to failure or close to it, rest about 20 seconds, and do as many more reps as you can; rest again and repeat. Each mini-set is short, two to five reps, but every rep is a hard one. It suits heavier work than drop sets do, sets of four to eight, on exercises you can rack and unrack safely.

Myo-reps

A close cousin of rest-pause, for higher reps: an activation set of 12 to 15, taken close to failure, then three to five deep breaths (about 15 seconds) and a short set of three to five reps at the same weight, and again, until the reps start to fall. Most of the work of three or four sets in a fraction of the time.

When to use them

  • At the end: after the working sets of an exercise, as the last word, not in place of them.
  • On safe exercises: machines, cables and isolation work. Not on heavy squats or deadlifts, where a failed rep is a dangerous one.
  • When time is short: they pack a lot of hard reps into a few minutes.
  • Sparingly: they're hard to recover from. A couple of exercises a session, on some days, beats every exercise every day.

In TrainingPlanner

The techniques are kinds of sets, planned after an exercise's working sets. Their load comes from the set before: a drop set starts about 20 % lighter than the weight you just lifted, rounded to plates, and rest-pause and myo-reps keep it. The rest timer knows them too: no rest before a drop, about 20 seconds before a rest-pause mini-set, 15 seconds before a myo-rep set.

  1. Open the program's board and tap the exercise.
  2. Tap Add sets and, under Pyramids and intensity techniques, pick Drop set, Rest-pause or Myo-reps.
  3. Open it to set how many: two drops, three rest-pause mini-sets or four myo-rep sets to start.
  4. Each one's page says how it's done, so you don't have to remember.

In the workout, each planned mini-set is a set of its own: same weight, its short rest before it, logged like any other. You can also turn any planned set into a different kind from its sheet when the day calls for it; a set made rest-pause that way is logged in parts, the reps of each mini-set one after the other.

Free or Pro. Drop sets, rest-pause, myo-reps and pyramids are part of TrainingPlanner Pro. A deload leaves them out.

Programs that use them

  • High-Frequency Push · Pull: one all-out set per exercise, with rest-pause on two days and drop sets and myo-reps on two others.
  • Push · Pull · Legs ×2: the volume days finish with all three.
  • Power & Hypertrophy: the hypertrophy days end with them.
  • Arm Specialization: drop sets on the volume day, rest-pause on the pump day.
  • Golden Era Split: drop sets to finish the supersets.
