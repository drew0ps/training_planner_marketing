Strength Wave: four-week waves at set percentages of your max

Fives, then threes, then a heavy single, then an easy week, and again a little heavier. Strength Wave trades the beginner's every-workout jump for a monthly one, which is what keeps an intermediate lifter getting stronger for years.

Who it's for

Strength Wave is for a lifter who has run a beginner program until the weights stopped moving every workout. It's built on the four big barbell lifts, one per day: the overhead press, the deadlift, the bench press and the squat, each with a little assistance work. Four days a week, about an hour each. It's what setup recommends to an intermediate lifter who wants to get stronger on four days.

The workouts

Each day is one main lift in the week's wave, then five sets of ten on it at a light weight, then two assistance exercises: chin-ups and dips with the press, leg raises and back extensions with the deadlift, rows and face pulls with the bench, leg curls and the ab wheel with the squat.

Week by week

The main lifts follow a four-week wave, as percentages of your one-rep max. The last set of each of the first three weeks is for as many reps as you can with good form (the app marks it with a +, the + set); the fourth week is a deload.

The percentages build in a training max of 90 %: week one's top set of five at 85 % of a training max is written as 76.5 % of your real max. That makes the heavy weeks heavy but never all-out, and leaves room to beat the numbers on the + sets. See training maxes.

How it progresses

Progress comes once a wave, not every workout. Each new wave loads its percentages from a max one step higher: 2.5 kg more on the press and the bench, 5 kg on the squat and the deadlift. By the third wave the bench is loaded from a max 5 kg over the one you entered, the squat and the deadlift from one 10 kg over, and the + sets show whether the bar agrees.

The assistance exercises climb by double progression as usual.

Start it in TrainingPlanner

  1. Open Programs, tap + and Start from a Template.
  2. Tap Template and pick Strength Wave under Intermediate. The picture at the top shows its cycle, phases and deloads.
  3. Its cycle is a week: the press, deadlift, bench and squat days on days 1, 2, 4 and 5. Change the training days if your week needs it, and tap Create.
  4. Enter your one-rep max for the four main lifts: setup asks for them, or set each under the exercise's Load › 1RM. Every percentage then has its weight.

Tips

  • Be honest with the max you enter. A max that's too high makes week three a grind and the waves stall; too low, and the + sets will tell you, with lots of reps.
  • Don't chase the + sets every week. A few good reps over the minimum is the job; a set to failure on a heavy deadlift is a risk the program doesn't ask for.
  • Raise a max yourself when the + sets are well over the minimum wave after wave: change it on the exercise's page and the next weeks follow.
