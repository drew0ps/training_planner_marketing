Training methods

Pendulum periodization: short swings between volume and intensity

Two weeks of volume, two weeks of heavy work, and back again, each swing a little heavier than the one before. The pendulum keeps size and strength both moving, which matters most when both move slowly.

Not published yet · 2 min read

How it swings

In block periodization the volume block comes first and the heavy work after it, once. That's a long time for an advanced lifter to go without heavy lifting, or without volume, and the quality left alone starts to slip. The pendulum shortens the blocks to a couple of weeks and alternates them: volume, intensity, volume, intensity. Each swing comes back a little heavier than the last, so the whole program still trends toward its heaviest work.

TrainingPlanner's pendulum is six phases of two cycles: three volume swings (4 × 10) and three intensity swings (5 × 5). Loads climb inside each swing and from one pair of swings to the next:

Pendulum, 12 cycles
CyclesSwingSets × repsLoadEffort
1–2Volume 14 × 1070 → 72.5 %RPE 8
3–4Intensity 15 × 580 → 82.5 %RPE 8
5–6Volume 24 × 1072.5 → 75 %RPE 8 → 9
7–8Intensity 25 × 582.5 → 85 %RPE 8 → 9
9–10Volume 34 × 1075 → 77.5 %RPE 9
11–12Intensity 35 × 585 → 87.5 %RPE 9

No deloads, by design

Phases of two cycles don't end in a deload in TrainingPlanner: a deload only ends phases of three cycles or more. The swings themselves give some relief, since the volume swings are lighter on the joints than the intensity swings before them. But twelve weeks with no easy week is a lot. Plan a deload between this program and the next, or lengthen the swings to three cycles and turn the deloads back on.

Who it's for

The pendulum is for advanced lifters, years in, chasing strength without giving up size. It's the model TrainingPlanner picks when you ask for automatic periodization with strength as the goal and advanced as your experience. Its reasoning, in the app's words: "With years behind you, short swings between volume and intensity keep both from fading while the peak builds."

It's a poor fit for beginners, who'd progress faster on almost anything simpler, and for programs of fewer than eight cycles, where there's no room for the pendulum to swing.

Set it up in TrainingPlanner

  1. In Programs, tap + › Build My Own › Structured Plan.
  2. Under Periodization, pick Pendulum as the model, or turn on Automatic and choose Get stronger and Advanced.
  3. Six swings of two cycles come with it. Add or remove swings to fit the weeks you have.
  4. Name it and tap Create, then plan the cycle on the board.
