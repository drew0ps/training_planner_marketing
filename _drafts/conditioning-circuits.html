Conditioning Circuits: three exercises back to back, little rest

Goblet squats, push-ups, cable rows, round again. Three exercises back to back with 45 seconds between them keep the heart rate up the whole session: conditioning and muscular endurance, with real weights in your hands.

Who it's for

Conditioning Circuits is for someone who wants to be fit as well as strong: more work capacity, better muscular endurance, and sessions that feel like exercise. It suits a lifter in a cutting phase, someone training for a sport or an event, or anyone who prefers moving to waiting between sets. Three days a week, 35 to 40 minutes each.

The workouts

Each workout is two circuits of three exercises: a leg movement, a push or a pull, and a third that keeps the heart rate up (mountain climbers, kettlebell swings, squat jumps, burpees). Go from one station to the next with little rest, then take a short break and go round again, three rounds per circuit.

How it progresses

By double progression, in high rep ranges: 12 to 15 for most stations, up to 30 for the conditioning moves. Add reps until every round reaches the top of a station's range, then a little weight. The bodyweight stations climb by reps.

The legs and glutes do the most work, from squats, lunges, step-ups, presses and swings; the upper body gets a push and a pull in every circuit. The real load is on the heart and lungs, which no set count shows: watch your training load, or record the workout on an Apple Watch for its heart rate.

Start it in TrainingPlanner

  1. Open Programs, tap + and Start from a Template.
  2. Tap Template and pick Conditioning Circuits under Intermediate. The picture at the top shows its cycle, phases and deloads.
  3. Its cycle is a week: Circuit A, B and C on days 1, 3 and 5. Change the training days if your week needs it, and tap Create.
  4. Start it from Today and pick moderate weights in the first session: you'll be tired by the third round, and the weights should still let you finish.

Tips

  • Set the stations up first. In a busy gym, a circuit only works if the three stations are near each other and free; pick swaps on the spot with Edit in the workout.
  • Pace the first round. Start at what you can hold for three rounds, not one.
  • Record it on your wrist. Start the workout on an Apple Watch and its heart rate and calories are saved to Health and shown with the workout.
