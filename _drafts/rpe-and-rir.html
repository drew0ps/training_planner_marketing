Training methods

RPE and RIR: how hard was that set?

Two sets of 100 kg for 5 can be completely different workouts: one with five more reps in the tank, one with none. Weight and reps don't say which. A number for the effort does, and it's one tap per set.

Not published yet · 4 min read

The two scales

RPE, the rating of perceived exertion, rates a set from 1 to 10 by how hard it was. Lifters use the top of the scale, tied to the reps you could still have done: 10 is a set with nothing left, 9 means one more rep was there, 8 means two, 7 three. A set you couldn't tell from a warm-up is 5 or below.

RIR, reps in reserve, counts those reps directly: RIR 2 is two left, RIR 0 none. It's the same number the other way round, RIR 2 is RPE 8, and some people find counting reps easier than rating effort. Use whichever you'll actually log; TrainingPlanner keeps the same number underneath, so switching changes the words and not your history.

RPERIRWhat it felt like
100Nothing left. Another rep would have failed.
91One more rep, just.
82Two more. Hard, and still fast enough.
73Three more. A solid working set.
64Four more. Brisk.
55+Easy: warm-up effort.

Half points help when a set is between two: RPE 8.5 is "maybe two more, definitely one". Rating gets more accurate with practice, and it's more accurate on hard sets than on easy ones, which are the sets where it matters.

Why log it

  • It measures the training. Muscles grow from sets taken close to failure. A set at RPE 9 and one at RPE 6 lift the same kilograms and do very different things. TrainingPlanner counts your effective sets by effort for exactly that reason.
  • It shows fatigue before the weights do. When the same weights take more and more effort, cycle after cycle, you need a deload. The app can only spot that if effort is logged.
  • It lets the plan follow the day. A heavy single at RPE 9 on a good day and a bad day are different weights, and that's the point.

Progression by effort

With RPE-based progression an exercise has an effort target, say RPE 8, and the weight follows what you logged against it. Last time a point or more easier than the target? The weight goes up a step. A point or more harder? It comes down a step. Within half a point, it's on target: the same again. This is how the main lifts of Squat · Bench · Deadlift move: a top triple at RPE 8, then back-off sets.

Effort also moves the weight within a workout. When a set comes in two points or more over its target, the next one starts about 5 % lighter; two points or more under it, with every rep done, and the next one starts a step heavier. The sheet says so: "Set 1 felt RPE 10 against 8: −5 % for this set". Programs that prescribe their loads, in percentages, are left as they are.

Log it in TrainingPlanner

  1. Pick the scale in Settings › Workouts › Effort as: RPE or RIR.
  2. In a workout, log a set as usual; its sheet has a row of efforts, 5 to 10 (or 5 down to 0 for RIR). Tap the one that fits.
  3. Between two? Tap +½ beside the row (−½ on RIR).
  4. Not sure what a level means? The ⓘ beside the row explains every one.

To progress an exercise by effort, open it from the program's Progression page, set RPE-based (RIR-based, on that scale) and pick its Effort target. The target also shapes the plan: a planned set counts against your weekly sets by the effort it aims for.

Free or Pro. Logging effort, on either scale, is in the free app, and so are the effective sets it counts. RPE-based progression, the next set moved by how the last one felt, and the next cycle written from your log are part of TrainingPlanner Pro.

Getting it right

  • Rate the set right after it, before the rest starts to make it feel easier than it was.
  • Count reps, not pain. Burning muscles at the end of a set of twenty isn't RPE 10 if you could have done three more.
  • Check yourself now and then. Take a set you'd call RPE 8 to the reps you thought you had left. If you got four more, your 8s are 6s, and your training is easier than you think.
  • Leave the near-failure sets for safe exercises. A machine or a dumbbell curl can go to RPE 10; a heavy squat without a spotter shouldn't.
