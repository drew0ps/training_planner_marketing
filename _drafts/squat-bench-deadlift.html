Squat · Bench · Deadlift: a top triple by effort, then back-off sets

Work up to a triple you could have done five of, then back off and do the volume. On a good day that triple is heavy; on a bad day it's lighter, and that's the point. The main lifts move by effort, not by a schedule.

Who it's for

Squat · Bench · Deadlift is for an experienced lifter whose goal is a bigger squat, bench press and deadlift, and who rates effort well: the program depends on it. Three days a week, one main lift each, about 50 minutes.

The workouts

Each day opens with its main lift: work up in small jumps to a top triple at RPE 8, a weight you could have lifted five times, then do back-off sets of five at about 85 % of it. The rest of the day supports the others: paused bench presses on squat day, presses and weighted chin-ups on bench day, front squats and rows on deadlift day.

How it progresses

The main lifts move by effort. Log the RPE of the top triple, and the next time the weight follows: a point or more easier than 8 and it goes up a step, a point or more harder and it comes down one, within half a point and it stays. The paused bench press and the front squat work the same way at RPE 7; the accessories climb by double progression.

Effort also steers a session as it goes: a back-off set that comes in two points over the target makes the next one start about 5 % lighter, and one two points under it, with every rep done, a step heavier. The sheet says why. On a strong day the weight follows you up; on a weak one, down.

Phases and deloads

Three phases, Volume, Strength and Peak, each ending with a deload. The sets stay the same through them, a top triple and back-offs, and the weights climb as the triples get easier. For phases that also change the sets and reps, apply a periodization model to the program.

Tips

  • Rate the triple honestly. An RPE 8 that's really a 9 sends the weight up when it should stay; check yourself now and then by finishing a set to its real limit, on a safe lift.
  • Work up in small jumps. The warm-up ramp shows the way to your first set; past it, a couple of singles help you judge the day.
  • Use RIR if it's easier. Two reps in reserve is the same target; switch the scale in Settings › Workouts.
