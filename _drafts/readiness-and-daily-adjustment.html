Training methods

Readiness: let last night's sleep and heart data shape today's training

The plan was written weeks ago; your night was last night. Readiness reads how you slept and how your heart recovered, against your own normal, and today's session takes it into account: never more than the plan, sometimes a little less.

Not published yet · 4 min read

Three signals, against your own normal

Readiness comes from three readings of the night before, which your watch, ring or band writes to Apple Health:

  • Heart rate variability (HRV): the variation between heartbeats. Higher than your usual means a nervous system that has recovered. It counts for half of the score.
  • Resting heart rate: lower than usual reads as readier. Three tenths of the score.
  • Sleep: more than usual reads as readier. The last fifth.

Each is compared with your normal over the four weeks before, not with anyone else's: an HRV of 40 ms can be a great night for one person and a poor one for another. That's also why readiness needs a week of nights before it shows a score: a normal takes that long to mean anything.

ScoreReadinessWhat it suggests
80 and upHighA good day to push: go for the top of your rep ranges.
60–79NormalTrain as planned.
40–59LowerTrain as planned, and stop a set short of where you'd usually go if it drags.
Under 40LowYour body looks run down: lighter work or a rest day may serve you better.

When one signal moved the score most, it says so: "HRV 12 % under your normal", for instance. And it's an estimate, never a measurement. A late dinner, a glass of wine or a hot room can move these numbers as much as training.

Training load

Readiness is the night; training load is the week. It counts every work set you logged by how hard it was (a set at RPE 8 is one unit, a set with no effort logged three quarters) and by what the exercise takes out of the whole body: a set of squats or deadlifts 1.3, a press or a row 1, a curl or a raise 0.7. Workouts other apps saved to Apple Health, a run or a ride, count by their length and heart rate, in the same units.

This week's load is then compared with your weekly average of the four weeks before: under 0.8 of it is fresh, up to 1.3 normal, up to 1.5 high, and above that very high, when the card suggests a lighter week or a deload soon.

Today's adjustment

When readiness or the load says so, the day's session is adjusted before it starts, and it says why:

  • Readiness lower: the presses, rows and the big lifts are held at last time's weights. No step up today; the curls and raises go on as planned.
  • Readiness low: every exercise is held, and the big lifts aim a point easier.
  • Load high: the squats, deadlifts and their kin are held; very high, the presses and rows too.
  • A muscle still recovering from earlier in the week: one set fewer on an exercise that trains it.

It never adds anything. A great night doesn't raise the plan, because one night's numbers are noisy and the plan already climbs. And it's always one tap away from Train as planned: you know things the numbers don't.

Turn it on

  1. In Settings, under Apple Health, turn on Read sleep and heart data for readiness and allow what Health asks for.
  2. Wear your watch, ring or band to bed. After a week of nights, Today on your iPhone shows a score. (Readiness is worked out where Apple Health is; the Mac shows your training load.)
  3. The Body tab shows the three signals against your normal, and the training load.
  4. On an adjusted day the workout opens with what was changed and why; tap Train as planned to set it aside.

Your data stays on your device. Readiness reads Health on the device and works there; the readings are never synced to iCloud or sent anywhere. Any wearable whose app writes HRV, resting heart rate and sleep to Apple Health works, Apple Watch or not.

Free or Pro. Readiness from Apple Health, other apps' workouts in your load, the adjustment from them, and the Readiness widget are part of TrainingPlanner Pro. The training load from your own logs, and one set fewer for a muscle still recovering, are free.
