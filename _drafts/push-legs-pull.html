Push · Legs · Pull: three sessions and a rest day, around and around

Push, legs, pull, rest, and around again. Each muscle comes back every fourth day, which is more often than a weekly split and still leaves room to recover.

Who it's for

Push · Legs · Pull is for an intermediate lifter whose goal is muscle: past the first months, comfortable with the main lifts, and able to train on most days. Pressing muscles get one day, the legs the next, pulling muscles the third, and the fourth is rest. Sessions take 45 to 60 minutes, kept short by supersets.

How a cycle looks

The cycle is four days, not a week: three sessions and a rest day. In a week that's five or six sessions, and every muscle is trained every fourth day, a little more than twice a week.

The workouts

Each session opens with one big lift done heavy (a bench press top set, belt squats, heavy pulldowns), then works through supersets: A1 then A2, rest, and again.

How it progresses

Everything moves by double progression. The bench press has a top set and back-off sets, each climbing in its own range: 5–7 for the top set, 8–10 for the back-offs. The belt squat ramps up in two sets of eight before its top set. The weighted pull-ups end with a set at bodyweight taken to failure: a back-off that goes as far as it goes, and shows on the day how much you have left.

The program comes with example weights, a 110 kg bench top set for one. Change them to yours on the board before you start, or in the first workout; from then on the app sets them from what you log.

Phases and deloads

Two phases, Accumulation and Intensification, of eight four-day cycles each: about four and a half weeks. The last cycle of each is a deload.

A high-volume program by any count: back, chest, shoulders and calves all land in the upper half of their weekly ranges. The hamstrings get one curl per cycle, which is the place to add something if you want more: a Romanian deadlift on the leg day.

Tips

  • Train on your days, not the calendar's. The rotation keeps its rhythm; if a day doesn't work, use Change day on Today to swap or move a session, and see what moves before it does.
  • Answer the three questions after each exercise. The next cycle adds a set where an exercise was easy and takes one off where it was too much. See exercise feedback.
  • Want it weekly? Change the cycle to seven days with Push, Legs and Pull on days 1, 3 and 5: less volume a week, more recovery.
  • Newer to lifting? The quick start's Push · Legs · Pull is the simple version: three days a week, no phases, and free as a repeating plan.
