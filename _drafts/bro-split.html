Bro Split: one muscle group a day, five days a week

Monday is chest day. Then back, shoulders, legs and arms, and the weekend off. The classic gym week: lots of sets for one muscle at a time, and each one gets a whole week to recover.

Who it's for

The Bro Split is for an intermediate lifter who trains to build muscle, likes a session focused on one part of the body, and can train five days a week. It has a name that makes people smile and a reputation it doesn't quite deserve: with enough sets for each muscle and progression that keeps asking for more, it works. Sessions take 40 to 55 minutes.

How a cycle looks

One week: chest, back, shoulders, legs and arms on days 1 to 5, two days off.

How it progresses

Everything moves by double progression: reps first, to the top of each range, then weight. Pull-ups and dips climb by reps until the top of the range, when the app suggests adding weight.

Phases and deloads

Two phases of four weeks, Accumulation and Intensification, each ending with a deload week.

The catch is frequency: each muscle gets its sets in one session a week. The chest, back and arms still reach good weekly volume, partly because the presses and rows train the arms on other days too. The legs get one day to cover quads, hamstrings, glutes and calves, which is where the split is thinnest. If legs matter to you, add a second short leg session on day 6.

Tips

  • Don't miss leg day. A missed day costs that muscle its whole week. Move it with Change day on Today rather than skipping it.
  • Use the last sets. With one session a week per muscle, every set counts: take them close to failure, and log how hard they were.
  • When it stops working, try a split that trains each muscle twice: Upper · Lower or Push · Pull · Legs · Upper · Lower.
