10×10 Volume: ten sets of ten, in supersets of opposing lifts

One hundred reps of one lift, with a weight you could lift twenty times, in ten sets of ten with barely a minute between them. It sounds easy for three sets. It isn't by the eighth. An old method, and a brutally effective one for size.

Who it's for

10×10 Volume is for an intermediate lifter after muscle, who has plateaued on ordinary sets and wants a hard block of volume to break out of it. It's short sessions of very high volume on a few lifts. Run it for a block or two, then go back to something with more variety; that's how it's meant to be used. Sessions take 45 to 50 minutes.

How a cycle looks

Three sessions in a five-day cycle: chest and back, legs and abs, a rest day, arms and shoulders, a rest day. That brings each session around a little more often than once a week.

The workouts

Each session has one pair of opposing lifts for ten sets of ten, done as a superset with 75 seconds' rest: incline dumbbell presses with pulldowns, squats with leg curls, dips with curls. Then a lighter superset of three sets to finish.

How it progresses

Start the ten-by-ten lifts with a weight you could lift about twenty times, about 60 % of your max, and keep it the same for all ten sets. The first sets feel easy; the last ones won't, and you may get seven or eight reps on them. Once all ten sets reach ten reps, the weight goes up a little next time: linear progression, 2.5 kg on the pulldown and the leg curl, 5 kg on the squat, 1 to 2 kg on the dumbbells. The finishing sets climb by double progression.

Phases and deloads

Two phases of seven cycles, about five weeks each, each ending with a deload: half the sets, about 10 % lighter. After this much volume, take them.

Over thirty sets a week for the chest, since the dips count for it as well as the triceps, and close to twenty for the back. That's well over the usual weekly ranges, by design: a short block of very high volume, then back to normal, is how this method works. Counted as effective sets, the early easy sets count for less, which is the honest number.

Tips

  • Don't raise the weight early. If the first five sets are easy, good: the last five are the program.
  • Keep the rests short. The superset's 75 seconds are part of the method. The rest timer runs by itself after each round.
  • Squats for ten sets of ten need a weight you're sure of; leg curls between them keep it bearable.
