Training methods

Your one-rep max, and training at a percentage of it

Some programs don't give you weights at all. They give you percentages: five reps at 75 %, a triple at 85 %. Of what? Your one-rep max, the most you can lift once. Enter it, and every set has its weight.

Not published yet · 4 min read

What the max is for

A one-rep max (1RM) is the heaviest weight you can lift once, with good form, on a given day. Few people need to know theirs for its own sake. But it's a yardstick: once it's known, "heavy" and "light" become numbers, and a program can say exactly how hard each set should be, for anyone, whatever they lift.

That's why percentage programs work so well for strength. Week one is five reps at 65 %, week three a single at 85 %, and the plan for a 60 kg bencher and a 160 kg bencher is the same plan.

Estimate it, don't test it

Testing a true max takes a good day, a warm-up, a spotter and some nerve, and it's tiring. For training, an estimate from a heavy set of a few reps is close enough. The usual one is Epley's formula:

Estimated 1RM = weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30). 100 kg for 5 comes out at about 117 kg.

Estimates are best from sets of two to six reps taken close to failure; from sets of twelve, they drift. The same formula, turned around, says roughly how many reps a percentage allows:

% of 1RMReps, aboutUsed for
951–2Peaking, singles
903Heavy triples
855Strength work
807Strength, volume
7510Muscle, volume
7013Muscle, technique
6020Endurance, speed work

"About" is doing work there: people differ, and so do lifts. Plenty of lifters get eight at 80 % on a squat and five on a bench press.

The training max

Many percentage programs don't use your true max but a training max a little below it, often 90 %. The heavy weeks still feel heavy, the reps are crisp, and a bad day doesn't wreck the plan. The program then raises the training max in small steps, wave after wave, whether or not you've tested.

In TrainingPlanner the training max is built into the percentages, so you always enter your real max. Strength Wave's top set of five at 85 % of a training max is written as 76.5 % of the max you enter.

Your maxes in TrainingPlanner

Every exercise has a 1RM under Load on its page. Until you enter one, the app estimates it from the heaviest of the exercise's planned sets, and shows the estimate. When you start a program that works in percentages, setup asks for the maxes it needs, one field per lift.

  1. Start a percentage program from Programs › + › Answer a Few Questions or from the templates.
  2. Setup's Your current maxes lists the lifts the program loads in percentages: enter each. A good recent set of three to five is enough to estimate from.
  3. Every percentage now has its weight, rounded to plates you can load.
  4. Later, change a max on the exercise's page, under Load › 1RM.

A program of several phases raises the max as it goes: each later phase loads its percentages from a max one step higher than the phase before, 2.5 kg on the presses and 5 kg on the squat and the deadlift by default. Periodization models load from your maxes too.

Free or Pro. The percentage programs are intermediate and advanced templates, and loads from your 1RM come with periodization: both are part of TrainingPlanner Pro. The estimated max of every exercise, and its best set, are in the free app.

Programs in percentages

  • Strength Wave: one main lift a day in four-week waves of fives, threes and a heavy single.
  • Strength Peak: twelve weeks from 70 % fives to 95 % singles, for a max attempt at the end.
  • Max Effort & Speed: fast sets at 50 to 60 % on its speed days.
