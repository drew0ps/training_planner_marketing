Muscle recovery, and the three questions after every exercise

A program can't know whether three sets of rows were too few for you or too many. You can. TrainingPlanner asks three questions after each exercise, the way a coach would, and writes the next cycle's sets from your answers.

Not published yet · 3 min read

Recovery, muscle by muscle

Every work set strains the muscles its exercise moves: harder sets more, the muscles that only help less (about 40 % of the strain), and the strain fades over the day to three days a muscle takes to recover, longer after more work. From the last week's logged sets, Today draws a body figure that glows warm where training still weighs and cools as each muscle recovers: Recovering, Nearly there or Ready.

It's an estimate, and you know your body better. So tell it: a muscle that feels ready sooner, or stays sore longer, than the figure says. Each answer nudges that muscle's recovery time by a tenth, and over weeks the estimate comes to fit you. A football match, a long run or a day of moving house can be added too: it strains the muscles it worked for a few days, like a workout.

On a day when a muscle is still recovering, today's session takes one set off an exercise that trains it, and says so. A muscle that's recovering keeps its training; it gets a little less of it.

The three questions

Once an exercise's sets are done, its card asks three things, the way a hypertrophy coach asks them:

  • The pump: low, moderate or high. How much the sets filled the muscle.
  • The workload: easy, about right or too much. How the sets felt to get through.
  • Last time: not sore, recovered, or still sore. Whether you'd recovered from the time before.

Pump and workload say whether the sets were enough; soreness says whether you recovered from them. When your best set came out below last time's, there's a fourth question: did you feel weaker, or did you do the reps more strictly? The numbers can't tell those apart, and only one of them is a reason to do less.

What the answers change

The next cycle's review reads them, exercise by exercise:

You saidNext cycle
Too muchOne set fewer
Still sore from the time beforeOne set fewer
Felt weaker than last timeOne set fewer (the same sets if it also felt easy)
Easy, or easy with little pumpOne set more
Little pump, not soreOne set more
Stricter form, not weakerThe same sets: the reps were worth more
Pump and workload about rightThe same sets

A set is only added while the muscle is under the top of its weekly range: "Felt easy, but the chest is at the top of its weekly range: the same sets." Every change is shown with its reason beside the exercise, and nothing changes until you accept it.

In TrainingPlanner

  1. Log an exercise's sets as usual. After the last one its card asks the three questions.
  2. Tap an answer in each row. The card folds away once all three are answered.
  3. At the end of the cycle, open the next cycle's review from Today: the answers show as reasons beside each exercise.
  4. Untick what you don't want and apply the rest.

Free or Pro. The recovery estimate, its body figure and one set fewer for a muscle still recovering are in the free app, and so are the three questions. The next cycle written from your answers and your log is part of TrainingPlanner Pro.
