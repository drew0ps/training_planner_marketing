Bodyweight Strength: weighted pull-ups, pistols and handstand push-ups

Twenty push-ups are easy now, and ten pull-ups too. The next step isn't more of them: it's pull-ups with a backpack full of books, squats on one leg, push-ups upside down. Strength and skill, with a pull-up bar and dip bars.

Who it's for

Bodyweight Strength is for someone past the basics of bodyweight training, who can do clean sets of pull-ups, dips and push-ups and wants to keep getting stronger without a barbell. You need a pull-up bar, dip bars (or two sturdy chairs), a wall, something to anchor your feet, and a belt or a backpack for added weight. Three days a week, about an hour each.

The workouts

Each session has a heavy pull and a heavy push with added weight, a hard single-leg squat, and core work: hanging leg raises, toes-to-bar or the ab wheel. Session A has the Nordic curl for the hamstrings, B handstand push-ups against a wall and archer push-ups, C jump squats and single-leg hip thrusts.

How it progresses

Two ways, by double progression. The weighted exercises (pull-ups, chin-ups, dips, push-ups) add reps to the top of their range, then 2.5 kg on the belt or in the backpack, and back to the bottom. The skill exercises (pistols, shrimp squats, handstand push-ups, archer push-ups, Nordic curls) add reps, and at the top of the range the app suggests a harder variation: less help from the box, the wall or the straight arm.

Not there yet on a skill? The notes say how to scale it: pike push-ups with the feet raised before handstand push-ups, sitting back to a box for the pistol.

Tips

  • Log the added weight, not your body weight. A weighted pull-up at 10 kg is 10 kg; 0 kg is a bodyweight set.
  • Treat the skills as practice. Stop a set of pistols or handstand push-ups when the form breaks; reps that fall over don't count.
  • Land the jumps softly. Reset between jump squats; it's a power exercise, not a cardio one.
