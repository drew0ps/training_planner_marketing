Training methods

Block periodization: accumulation, transmutation, realization

Instead of training everything a little, train one thing a lot, then the next, each block building on the one before: volume, then strength, then a short peak. It's how many advanced lifters and athletes plan their year.

Not published yet · 3 min read

Three blocks, one direction

A block is a phase that concentrates on one quality and lets the others ride. Three of them, in order, make the classic sequence:

  • Accumulation: high volume at moderate loads. It builds muscle and work capacity, the base the next blocks draw on. The longest block.
  • Transmutation: heavier, more specific strength work. The volume comes down, the load goes up, and the muscle from the first block is turned into force on the lifts that matter.
  • Realization: a short block near your max, with little volume. Fatigue clears, strength shows, and you test, compete or set a new best.

The idea behind it is that a quality you trained hard lasts for a while after you stop: muscle built in accumulation is still there weeks later, while strength work turns it into force. So the blocks can follow each other instead of all sharing every week, and each gets enough work to make a difference.

What TrainingPlanner writes

The block model's default program is nine cycles: four of accumulation, three of transmutation, two of realization. The blocks get shorter as they get heavier, because heavy work is harder to recover from:

Realization has no deload: only phases of three cycles or more end in one, and a two-cycle peak is light enough already. Take an easy week after it, before the next program.

Who it suits

Blocks are for lifters who've outgrown simple programs. A beginner doesn't need them: almost any training works on every quality at once. An intermediate lifter whose goal is strength is exactly who TrainingPlanner gives the block model when it chooses for you; an advanced one gets the pendulum, which swings between volume and intensity more often so neither fades.

Several of the app's programs are built in blocks:

  • Squat · Bench · Deadlift: volume, strength and peaking phases, the main lifts moved by effort.
  • Strength Peak: fives at 70–75 %, triples at 80–85 %, then doubles and singles up to 95 %.
  • Bench Specialization: a volume block, then an intensity block with fewer reps and more weight.
  • Athletic Power: a strength block, then a power block that turns it into speed.

Set it up in TrainingPlanner

  1. In Programs, tap + › Build My Own › Structured Plan.
  2. Pick the cycle: plan a new one, or start from one of yours or a template's.
  3. Under Periodization, tap Model and pick Block. Accumulation, Transmutation and Realization come with it, 4, 3 and 2 cycles.
  4. Lengthen a block if you like, name the program and tap Create.
