Max Effort & Speed: heavy days, fast days, and lifts that rotate

Two days a week you lift as heavy as the day allows, on a variation that changes every few weeks. Two days a week you lift light and fast. Heavy for force, fast for speed: together they build strength that keeps going up for advanced lifters.

Who it's for

Max Effort & Speed is for an advanced lifter chasing strength: years in, with a squat and a bench that no longer move on simple programs. Its method, often called conjugate training, trains the two ends of strength on different days: maximal force on heavy days, and rate of force on fast ones. Four days a week, under an hour.

The workouts

On the max-effort days, work up in small jumps to a heavy single or triple with one rep left, RPE 9, then two back-off triples. The lift changes every phase, so you're always close to your best on something without grinding the same lift into the ground: a box squat, then a deficit deadlift, then a safety-bar squat for the lower body; a floor press, then a close-grip bench press, then an incline bench press for the upper.

On the speed days, the box squat and the bench press are light and fast: twelve doubles and nine triples at 50 to 60 % of your max, a minute between sets, every rep as fast as you can make it. Three grip widths on the bench spread the work.

How it progresses

The max-effort lifts go by effort: rate the top single or triple, and the next time the weight follows how it felt against RPE 9. A new lift each phase starts a new record to beat. The speed lifts follow their percentages; enter your squat and bench one-rep max under each speed lift's Load › 1RM, and later phases load from a max a step higher. The accessories climb by double progression.

Phases and deloads

Three phases of four weeks: a strength wave, a speed wave and a heavy wave, each ending with a deload week.

Tips

  • Speed means speed. If a rep on a speed day slows down, the weight is too heavy for the day's job. Every rep should look the same.
  • Max effort is not all-out. RPE 9 is one rep left. The heavy day builds strength by being heavy, not by being a test.
  • Box squats need a box at about parallel; sit back to it, pause, and drive up without rocking.
