High-Frequency Push · Pull: short, all-out sessions, six days a week

Four exercises, one all-out set each, half an hour, every day but one. Little volume and nothing left in the tank: each muscle trained three times a week, with so little to recover from that it can be.

Who it's for

High-Frequency Push · Pull is for an experienced lifter, training without drugs, who grows on frequency and effort rather than volume, or who simply has half an hour a day rather than ninety minutes three times a week. Every set is taken to failure, so it's for someone who knows how to get there safely. Six days a week, 25 to 30 minutes each.

  2. Day 2Pull (Heavy)

The workouts

Push days train the quads, chest, shoulders and triceps; pull days the hamstrings, back and biceps. Each session is four exercises, and each exercise is one all-out work set after the warm-up, made harder in a different way through the week:

  • Days 1–2, heavy: a set of four to six to failure, then rest-pause: fifteen seconds, a few more reps, fifteen seconds, a few more.
  • Days 3–4, slow: a set of six to eight with five seconds down and two seconds in the stretched position, the last rep held in the stretch as long as you can. A tempo of 5-2-1-0.
  • Days 5–6, pump: drop sets on the compound lifts, two drops of about 20 % with no rest, and myo-reps on the isolations.

How it progresses

Each work set climbs by double progression: reps to the top of its range, then weight. The rest-pause, drop and myo-rep sets start from the set before them, so they rise with it. Each day's version of an exercise keeps its own numbers, since the app compares a set with the last time that exercise was done for the same reps.

Phases and deloads

Two phases of six weeks, Base and Build, each ending with a deload week, which leaves the techniques out.

On paper it's a low-volume program: seven to fourteen work sets a week per muscle. But every one is a set to failure with a technique after it, so each counts in full as an effective set. Short sessions, often: the muscle is trained three times a week and never has a big session to recover from.

Start it in TrainingPlanner

  1. Open Programs, tap + and Start from a Template.
  2. Tap Template and pick High-Frequency Push · Pull under Advanced. The picture at the top shows its cycle, phases and deloads.
  3. Its cycle is a week: push and pull in turn on days 1 to 6, heavy, then slow, then pump. Change the training days if your week needs it, and tap Create.
  4. Start it from Today. In the first week, warm up to a weight you could do for the top of the range, and take it to failure.

Tips

  • Failure, safely. On squats and bench presses, use safety arms or a spotter; on the slow days, stop when the tempo breaks.
  • Warm up properly. With one work set, the warm-up is most of the session's time, and it's what makes the one set possible.
  • Log the effort. Every set here is RPE 10 by design; log it, and the app counts it as the whole set it is.
