Strength Peak: twelve weeks to a max attempt on squat, bench and deadlift

Fives, then triples, then doubles and singles, each block heavier than the last, and an easy week at the end so you arrive at test day fresh. Twelve weeks, three lifts, one goal: a new max.

Who it's for

Strength Peak is for an advanced lifter with a date: a meet, a test, or a max attempt they've been building to. It's built on the squat, the bench press and the deadlift, with a lighter, paused bench day and the accessories each lift needs. Four days a week, about an hour each.

The workouts

Each main lift has its day, loaded in percentages of your max; the fourth day presses lighter, paused on the chest, by feel at RPE 7. Accessories after each main lift support it: leg presses and curls after the squat, rows and close-grip presses after the bench, front squats and pulldowns after the deadlift.

Week by week

Three blocks of four weeks. Each has three working weeks that climb, then a deload. The deadlift gets fewer sets than the squat and the bench, since it's the hardest to recover from:

In the peak block the accessories are trimmed to two sets each, so all the energy goes into the main lifts. The last week is a deload; test the week after it.

How it progresses

The main lifts follow their percentages, set by the max you enter, and every percentage block loads from a max one step higher than the block before: 5 kg on the squat and the deadlift, 2.5 kg on the bench. The paused bench moves by effort, the accessories by double progression.

Start it in TrainingPlanner

  1. Open Programs, tap + and Start from a Template.
  2. Tap Template and pick Strength Peak under Advanced. The picture at the top shows its cycle, phases and deloads.
  3. Its cycle is a week: squat and bench on days 1 and 2, deadlift and the light bench day on 4 and 5. Count back twelve weeks from your test day to start. Change the training days if your week needs it, and tap Create.
  4. Enter your one-rep max for the squat, the bench press and the deadlift: setup asks for them, or set each under the exercise's Load › 1RM.

Tips

  • Enter a max you've actually lifted, recently. The peak's 95 % singles are only as safe as the max they come from.
  • Don't test early. The singles in week three of the peak are not a test; the test comes after the deload, when the fatigue is gone.
  • On test day, warm up as you would for a heavy single and take three attempts: one you're sure of, one at about your old max, one for a new one. A new best gets its trophy as you log it.
