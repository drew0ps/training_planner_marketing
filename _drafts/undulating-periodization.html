Training methods

Undulating periodization: daily (DUP) and weekly (WUP)

Heavy on Monday, light on Wednesday, in between on Friday. Or heavy this week, lighter next week, lighter again the week after. Undulating plans change the rep range often, so no quality is ever left alone for long.

Not published yet · 3 min read

Why undulate

A linear plan trains one quality at a time: weeks of volume, then weeks of strength. That's fine for a while, but qualities you stop training fade, and an advanced lifter gains slowly enough that weeks without one start to cost. Undulating plans keep all of them in play by changing the rep range often, and add to each from one round to the next.

There are two ways to do it, which differ only in how often the range changes.

Daily undulating (DUP)

Every cycle has a session for each quality. In TrainingPlanner's model, the first session of a cycle is hypertrophy (4 × 10 at 70 % of your max), the second power (6 × 2 at 75 %, fast, at RPE 6), the third strength (5 × 5 at 80 %), and then around again if the cycle has more sessions. From one cycle to the next every session gets 2.5 % heavier, and the last cycle of each phase is a deload.

Each lift gets practiced three times a week at three different loads, which suits lifters who recover well and like variety. It's the model TrainingPlanner picks for an advanced lifter whose goal is muscle or general fitness.

Undulating by cycle (WUP)

Here a whole cycle shares one rep range, and the range rotates from one cycle to the next: hypertrophy, strength, power, then around again a little heavier. It's usually done week by week, which is where its other name comes from, but any cycle length works. The default program is two phases of six cycles, each phase two full rotations, the last cycle a deload.

WUP is gentler than DUP, since every session in a week trains the same way, and it gives a clear rhythm to the weeks. TrainingPlanner picks it for an intermediate lifter whose goal is muscle: rotating the rep range from cycle to cycle keeps size coming once simple adding stalls.

Programs built on them

  • Full Body Undulating: the same main lifts three times a week, heavy (4–6 reps), medium (8–10) and light (12–15).
  • Rep Range Waves: a heavy week, a moderate week, a high-rep week and a deload, in three waves.
  • Power & Hypertrophy: two heavy power days and three hypertrophy days every week.

Apply it in TrainingPlanner

  1. Open a structured plan and, under Periodization, tap Model.
  2. Pick Daily undulating (DUP) or Undulating by cycle (WUP). The chart shows each session's load and focus, cycle by cycle.
  3. Tap Apply to all cycles and confirm: every cycle's sets, reps and loads are written from the model.
  4. The outline under the chart says what each phase gets: each focus with its sets, reps and load.
