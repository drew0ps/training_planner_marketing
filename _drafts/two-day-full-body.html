Two-Day Full Body: strength and muscle on two days a week

Two days a week is enough to get stronger and build muscle, if both days count. Two full-body workouts with a few days between them, each covering every major movement, with enough sets to grow on.

Who it's for

Two-Day Full Body is for anyone who can only train twice a week (work, family, a sport on the other days) and wants the most out of those two. It's a beginner program, but it suits anyone at that schedule. Each workout takes about an hour, because it has to cover the whole body properly.

How a cycle looks

A week, with the two workouts three days apart: day 1 and day 4. Monday and Thursday, or Tuesday and Friday.

The workouts

Each workout has a squat or deadlift, a press, a back exercise and a second leg exercise, then a short superset for the arms and shoulders and one for the core. Workout A squats and benches; workout B deadlifts and presses overhead.

How it progresses

By double progression: reps at the same weight to the top of each range, then a step heavier. With only two sessions, each lift comes around once a week, so progress is steady rather than fast.

Phases and deloads

Two phases of six weeks, Base and Build, each ending with a deload week.

Fewer sets a week than a three- or four-day program, which is the honest cost of two days; what's there is spread over every muscle, and the big lifts carry most of it. Taking sets close to failure matters more here, since each one has to count.

Start it in TrainingPlanner

  1. Open Programs, tap + and Start from a Template.
  2. Tap Template and pick Two-Day Full Body under Beginner. The picture at the top shows its cycle, phases and deloads.
  3. Its cycle is a week: Full Body A on day 1, Full Body B on day 4. Change the training days if your week needs it, and tap Create.
  4. Start it from Today and pick your weights in the first two workouts: ones you could lift two or three more times than the bottom of each range.

On iPad and Mac

The same program with more of it on screen: the iPad and the Mac lay the board out day by day, side by side, and your programs and workouts sync between them through your private iCloud.

Free or Pro. Two-Day Full Body is a beginner template, so the free app runs it as a repeating plan: in Programs, tap + › Build My Own › Repeating Plan and start from the Two-Day Full Body cycle. The weights then start from what you lifted last time, and you add the step yourself. The structured program, with its phases, its deloads and the progression done for you, is part of TrainingPlanner Pro.

Tips

  • Missed a day? Move it rather than skip it: Change day on Today pushes it to tomorrow or another day, and shows what moves.
  • Walk, cycle, play on the other days. They don't get in the way of two lifting days; with Pro, the workouts other apps save to Apple Health count in your training load.
  • A third day? Add one, or switch to a three-day program like Full Body Undulating.
