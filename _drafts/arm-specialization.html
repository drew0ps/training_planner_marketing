Arm Specialization: six weeks that put the arms first

For six weeks, the arms come first: biceps and triceps three times a week, heavy, then for volume, then for the pump, while everything else holds its ground on less. A specialization block works because it's short.

Who it's for

Arm Specialization is for an intermediate lifter whose arms lag behind the rest, or who simply wants bigger arms, and is happy to put other goals on hold for six weeks. Four days a week, under an hour each. Afterwards, go back to a balanced program; the arms keep most of what they gained on normal volume.

The workouts

Three of the four days train the arms in a different way. The heavy day pairs the bench press with a close-grip bench pyramid and heavy curls and dips. The volume day pairs curls and triceps extensions in supersets, finishing with drop sets. The pump day runs cable curls and pressdowns with rest-pause. The legs get a day of their own to hold their strength.

How it progresses

Most exercises climb by double progression; the close-grip bench pyramid by linear progression, each step up once you get all its reps. The dips are done with added weight, a belt or a dumbbell between the feet, and climb by reps first.

Phases and deloads

One phase of six weeks; the sixth week is a deload. Then move on: specialization works best as a block.

Twenty-six work sets a week for the triceps and twenty for the biceps, more than most programs give them in two. The rest of the body gets enough to keep its strength, not to grow: that's the trade a specialization block makes.

Tips

  • Mind the elbows. Three arm days a week is a lot for the joints. Warm them up, and swap an exercise that hurts for one that doesn't, from the workout's Edit.
  • Squeeze the pump day. Light weights, short rests and rest-pause: the burn is the point.
  • Track your arms. With Pro, log an arm measurement in the Body tab at the start and the end; the program's summary shows the before and after.
