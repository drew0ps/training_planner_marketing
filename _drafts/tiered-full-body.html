Tiered Full Body: a heavy lift, a volume lift and a light one in every session

Every session has three tiers: one heavy lift for strength, one moderate lift for volume, one light exercise for high reps. Four sessions rotate the big lifts through the tiers, so each is trained heavy once and for volume once.

Who it's for

Tiered Full Body suits a beginner who has the basic lifts down and wants a bit more from each session than three sets of five: some heavy work, some volume, some pump. It's built on the squat, the bench press, the overhead press and the deadlift, with pulldowns and rows to keep the back in step. Sessions are short, about 45 minutes, every other day.

How a cycle looks

Four sessions on alternate days make an eight-day cycle: A1, B1, A2, B2, each followed by a rest day.

  1. Day 1Full Body A1
  2. Day 2Rest
  3. Day 3Full Body B1
  4. Day 4Rest
  5. Day 5Full Body A2
  6. Day 6Rest
  7. Day 7Full Body B2
  8. Day 8Rest

The workouts

Tier 1 is heavy: five sets of three, the last for as many reps as you can. Tier 2 is volume at a moderate weight: three sets of ten. Tier 3 is light and high-rep, 15 to 25, with the last set pushed hard. The squat is tier 1 in A1 and tier 2 in A2; the bench press the other way round. The overhead press and the deadlift swap the same way in the B sessions.

How it progresses

Tiers 1 and 2 move by linear progression: get every rep and that lift is a step heavier next time it's in the same tier, 5 kg on the squat and the deadlift, 2.5 kg on the presses. Tier 3 climbs by double progression: reps first, up to 25, then a step heavier.

The last set of tier 1 is a test: as many reps as you can, with good form. Many reps over the three is a sign the lift has room to climb; just three, and the next jump may be the last for a while.

Phases and deloads

Two phases of six cycles, about seven weeks each. The last cycle of each is a deload: half the sets, about 10 % lighter.

With the squat and the deadlift each trained heavy and for volume, the glutes, hamstrings and lower back get a lot of work; the chest and shoulders get the presses. Arms get none on their own: add a set of curls at the end if you want them.

Tips

  • Start the heavy tier conservatively. Five sets of three with a weight you could do five times leaves room to climb for weeks.
  • Count the last set's reps honestly. Stop when the form breaks, not when the muscle does.
  • Log how hard the sets were. An RPE on the heavy sets shows when the climb gets steep before you start missing reps.
