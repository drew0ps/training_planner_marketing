5×5 Strength: five sets of five on the big barbell lifts

Two workouts, A and B, three times a week. Squat every time, five sets of five, and a little more on the bar whenever you got every rep. Few programs are this simple, and few make a new lifter stronger faster.

Who it's for

5×5 Strength is for someone in their first year of lifting, or coming back after a long break, who wants to get stronger on the five lifts that matter most: the squat, the bench press, the overhead press, the barbell row and the deadlift. You need a barbell, a rack, a bench and plates, three days a week with a day off between them, and about an hour.

It starts light on purpose. Every lift begins at or near the empty bar, so the first weeks are practice and the weight climbs fast. If you've never squatted or deadlifted, have someone who knows the lifts watch your first sessions. The app shows how each lift is done, step by step, but it can't see you.

How a cycle looks

There are two workouts, and they take turns: A, B, A one week, B, A, B the next. That's why the app plans 5×5 Strength as a 14-day cycle with six workouts in it, so both weeks of the rhythm are in the plan.

The workouts

Workout A is the squat, the bench press and the row. Workout B is the squat again, the overhead press, and one heavy set of five on the deadlift after warming up: after five sets of squats, one set is enough, and a deadlift takes longer to recover from than the other lifts.

How it progresses

Every lift moves by linear progression: complete every rep of every set and the next time you do that lift, it's a step heavier. Miss a rep, and it stays where it was until you get them all. The steps are 2.5 kg for the squat, the bench press and the row, 5 kg for the deadlift, and 1.25 kg for the overhead press, which stalls first and does better with small jumps.

The squat is in both workouts, so it goes up every time you train: three steps, 7.5 kg, in a good week. The bench press and the overhead press take turns, so each goes up every other workout. That's a lot of weight in a few weeks, and it's why the program works for a beginner and doesn't for long after: the body adapts faster in the first year than it ever will again.

You don't keep count. When you start a set, the weight is already where it should be, with the reason beside it ("All reps completed last time: +2.5 kg", or "Missed the target last time: repeat 60 kg × 5").

Phases and deloads

The program runs for two phases of ten weeks. The last cycle of each is a deload: half the work sets at about 10 % less weight, two easier weeks for the joints and the nervous system before the next run of heavier ones.

Where the work goes

Counted in work sets a week, the program is about the legs and the hips: the squat is in every workout and trains the quads and glutes, the deadlift adds to the glutes and hamstrings. Chest, back and shoulders get the bench press, the row and the press. There is no work for the arms on their own; they work along in the presses and the row.

That's on purpose: the program buys strength on a few lifts with as little else as possible. If you want more for your arms or your shoulders, a set or two at the end of a workout won't get in its way.

Tips

  • Warm up into the first set. Once the bar is 30 kg or more, the first set shows a warm-up ramp beside the weight: about 40, 60 and 80 % of it for 8, 5 and 3 reps.
  • Tell the app your plates. In Settings › Equipment, say which plates your gym has; the weights round to what you can load, and the plate calculator shows each side.
  • Rest long enough. The template rests 3 minutes between sets. When the sets get heavy, take more: it's a strength program, and a rushed set is a missed one.
  • Log how hard the sets were. An RPE on your sets lets Today notice when effort keeps climbing while the lifts fall back, and suggest a deload before you'd have noticed yourself.
  • Know when it's done. When the weights stop moving even after a deload, adding weight every workout has run its course. Programs for intermediates move in weekly or monthly waves instead: Strength Wave, Upper · Lower or Squat · Bench · Deadlift.
