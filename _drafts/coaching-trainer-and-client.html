Coaching: how a trainer and a client work together in TrainingPlanner

A report goes one way, a program comes back the other. The client sends their trainer one file with their plans and every set of the last weeks; the trainer opens it in TrainingPlanner, adjusts the plan, and sends a file back. No accounts, no server: only the two of you, and whatever you already use to send each other things.

Not published yet · 6 min read

How it works

  1. The client sends a report. One file with their programs, every set of the weeks they pick, and a note, sent any way they like: Messages, Mail, AirDrop, a chat app.
  2. The trainer prepares. They open the file in TrainingPlanner and see the client's workouts set by set, what moved and what didn't; then they adjust the client's program or write a new one.
  3. The client takes what comes back. They open the trainer's file and see every change before taking it. A program they already follow keeps its start date and its history.

Nothing goes through a server, and neither of you needs an account. The files go only to whom you send them, and what you keep stays on your devices and in your own iCloud. In this post, Alex follows the simple Push · Legs · Pull and works with Sam, a trainer.

For the client

Send a report

  1. Open Settings › Coaching and tap Send a report.
  2. Type your name: it's how your trainer sees you in their client list.
  3. Pick the workouts to include: the last 4 weeks, the last 3 months or everything. Add a note: what felt good, what hurt, what you'd like to change.
  4. Choose whether your body measurements and recovery notes go too.
  5. Tap Send and pick how: a message, mail, AirDrop. The report counts as sent once it has gone; close the share sheet instead and nothing is sent.

A report holds your programs as they're planned, every set of every workout in the period with its effort and your notes, and the summaries of programs you finished. With your say-so it also carries your body entries (weight, body fat and the tape, never photos) and what you told the recovery figure: the muscles that felt ready sooner or stayed sore. What your trainer sees first shows the top of the report as Sam reads it.

Coaching remembers when the last report went out. Two weeks later, or once new programs came in after it, Coaching and Today say it's time to send another.

Take what your trainer sends

  1. Tap the file where it arrived, in Messages, Mail or Files. TrainingPlanner opens it. (Or Coaching › Open a file from my trainer.)
  2. Read the changes: each one is listed under the program it changes.
  3. Tap Apply changes for an adjusted program, Add to programs for a new one, Save to my cycles for a cycle; or Take everything.

An adjusted program keeps everything that's yours: when it started, the days you moved, the changes you made during workouts and every logged workout. A new program joins Programs, and becomes your current one if you have none. A cycle goes to Programs › Cycle Library, to start as a repeating plan or add to a program.

Free for clients. Sending reports and taking programs is free, and a program from your trainer keeps its automatic progression and pyramids without Pro. Following a structured plan (phases of cycles) needs TrainingPlanner Pro, and so does adding a second repeating plan beside your own.

For the trainer

Turn on trainer mode

  1. Open Settings › Coaching and turn on I coach clients.
  2. Your clients are under Clients, there and at the top of Today.
  3. Wait for a client's report, or add a client by hand to send them a program first. Their report is matched to them by name when it arrives.

Read a client's report

  1. Tap the client's file. The report opens: who it's from, their note and what's in it.
  2. Tap Add to my clients. A newer report from the same client replaces the old one; your drafts for them stay.
  3. The client's page opens: the period and their note, workouts done and missed, work sets and volume, and the highlights. Below: their current program, the exercises that moved most, their body and recovery, and the workouts themselves.
  4. Tap a workout for every set: what was lifted, at what effort, against what was aimed for, and against the same exercise the time before.

Adjust the program and send it back

  1. On the client's page, tap their current program, then Adjust.
  2. The draft opens like any program of yours: its board, its exercises and sets, its progression. Change what needs changing; here, the seated shoulder press becomes a landmine press for Alex's shoulder.
  3. Back on the client's page, the draft is under To send, with the number of changes.
  4. Under With the file, add your name and a note.
  5. Tap Send at the bottom and send it the way the report came.

Besides adjusting a client's program, Send a program sends one of your own programs, a new repeating or structured plan, or one of the app's programs, and Send a cycle a single cycle: a week away from the gym, a block of accessory work. In trainer mode, any program or cycle in Programs has Send to a client too. Everything under To send goes in the next file.

On the Mac and iPad

Trainer mode works the same on the Mac and the iPad, with more room for a client's page and a program's board. Your clients are kept in your iCloud, so they're on every device you sign in on.

Good to know

  • Nothing to set up. No accounts, no invitations, no server: a client and a trainer only need the app and a way to send each other a file.
  • Privacy. A report goes only to whom the client sends it. A trainer keeps their clients' reports on their own devices and in their own iCloud. Photos never leave the client's phone.
  • Removing a client deletes their report and your drafts for them. Nothing changes on the client's device.
  • A file meant for someone else says so when it's opened: changes prepared for another athlete's program come in as new programs, never over yours.

Free or Pro. Clients send reports and take programs for free. Trainer mode, keeping clients and sending them programs, is part of TrainingPlanner Pro.
