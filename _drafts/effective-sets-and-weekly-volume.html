Training methods

Effective sets: counting training volume the way muscles do

Ten easy sets and ten hard ones move the same kilograms, and they aren't the same training. What builds muscle is sets taken close to failure, so that's what TrainingPlanner counts: every work set, by how hard it was.

Not published yet · 3 min read

Why not count kilograms

The classic measure of training volume is tonnage: sets × reps × weight. It's easy to work out and mostly misleading. A set of 20 at 50 kg has more tonnage than a set of 5 at 100 kg, and they train different things. Ten sets of an easy warm-up weight have plenty, and build nothing.

What research on muscle growth points to is simpler: the number of sets taken close to failure, per muscle, per week. The reps that make a muscle grow are the last hard ones, when every fibre has to work; a set that stops five reps short never gets there. So the useful count isn't how much you lifted, but how many hard sets each muscle got.

Effective sets

TrainingPlanner counts every work set by how close to failure it was, from the effort you logged:

Effort loggedCounts as
RPE 9 or 10 (one rep left, or none)1 set
RPE 8 (two left)0.85
RPE 7 (three left)0.7
RPE 6 (four left)0.5
Easier0.3
No effort logged0.7

A half point counts halfway between its neighbours: RPE 8.5 is 0.925. The values are an estimate of what the research on training close to failure suggests, not a measurement, but they're far closer to the truth than counting every set as one. Each set counts for every muscle its exercise trains as a prime mover; the muscles that only help get no sets from it.

How many a week

Most people grow on about 10 to 20 effective sets a week for a muscle, fewer for small muscles that work along in the big lifts. TrainingPlanner keeps a range per muscle:

The lower end is where growth reliably happens; the top is where most people stop gaining from more. New lifters need less: setup starts a beginner on ranges about a third lower. Lifters who recover well, or who are specializing, can use more.

Where you see them

  • On the board: the work sets per muscle of the cycle you're planning, so you see what it leaves out before you train it. The planner names the muscles a cycle plans too few or too many sets for.
  • On the calendar: this week's effective sets per muscle against each one's range, and the muscles under or over it.
  • After a workout: its effective sets, and each muscle's, in how it went.
  • In the next cycle's review: a set added for an exercise that was easy stops at the top of its muscle's range.
  1. Open Settings and, under Weekly effective sets per muscle, tap Per muscle.
  2. Pick a preset (Lower, Usual or Higher) or set a muscle's range with its buttons.
  3. On the Calendar, the week's sets per muscle are above the month.
  4. On a program's board, tap the work sets line for every muscle's sets in the cycle.

A plan that names no effort target is counted as if its sets are taken close to failure: one set each. A set planned at RPE 8 counts 0.85, so a plan and the week you train it read the same.
